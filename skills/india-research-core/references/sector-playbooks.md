# Sector-specific research and valuation

Choose the closest economic model and record business mix. Multi-business groups
may need sum-of-the-parts. These are analytical lenses, not fixed rating formulas.
Confirm actual sector definitions and current regulatory metrics in disclosures.

| Sector | Operating and financial priorities | Valuation lens / traps |
|---|---|---|
| Banks | Loan/deposit mix and growth, deposit pricing, NIM, slippages, GNPA/NNPA, provision coverage, credit costs, ROA/ROE, liquidity and capital adequacy | P/B linked to sustainable ROE and cost of equity, residual income or equity cash flows; bank deposits are operating funding, not ordinary EV debt |
| NBFC/HFC | AUM and disbursements, funding mix/cost, ALM, spreads, delinquency vintages, provisions, leverage, capital | P/B and earnings on normalized credit costs; assess refinancing and liquidity stress |
| Life insurance | APE, VNB and margin, embedded value, persistency, product mix, distribution, solvency | Embedded value and VNB economics; ordinary P/E can mislead |
| General insurance | Premium mix, combined/loss ratio, reserves, underwriting profitability, investment income, solvency | Normalized earnings and book value; reserve releases and catastrophe cycles |
| AMCs / exchanges | Flows, AUM/volumes, market share, take rates/yields, mix, operating leverage, regulation | Earnings/FCF, cash adjustment; distinguish market appreciation from organic growth |
| IT services | Constant-currency growth, deal wins and conversion, client concentration, utilization, attrition, subcontracting, pricing | Earnings/FCF and DCF; FX gains, contract announcements versus realized revenue |
| FMCG / consumer | Volume versus price growth, distribution, market share, gross margins, ad spend, working capital | DCF and normalized multiples; premium assumptions must match growth durability |
| Retail / platforms | Same-store growth, unit economics, mature cohorts, store economics, retention, contribution margins, lease burden, dilution | Lease-consistent EV metrics, cash flows or scenario valuation; GMV is not revenue and adjusted EBITDA is not cash |
| Autos / ancillaries | Volumes, mix, cycle position, capacity, commodity costs, technology transition, customer concentration | Mid-cycle earnings/FCF; financing subsidiaries and supplier/customer concentration |
| Pharma / healthcare | Product/geographic mix, approvals and inspections, pipeline, pricing erosion, R&D, hospital occupancy/ARPOB where relevant | Normalized earnings/DCF or asset scenarios; one-off launches and regulatory concentration |
| Industrials / capital goods / defence | Order book quality, execution, advances, receivables, margins, capacity, customer budgets, cash conversion | Execution-based forecasts; avoid treating order book as guaranteed profit |
| Infrastructure / utilities | Project concessions, regulated returns, utilization, receivables, tariffs, capex, leverage, contingent obligations | Project DCF/SOTP and sustainable cash flows; finite concessions and project debt |
| Metals / mining / commodities | Cost curve, reserves, realizations, capacity, leverage, capex, inventory and commodity cycle | Mid-cycle EV/EBITDA/asset value or cycle DCF; low peak-cycle P/E can be expensive |
| Oil / gas / chemicals | Segment mix, feedstock costs, spreads, refining/marketing cycle, regulation, inventory effects, capex | Segment/cycle valuation; distinguish demand-led from supply-led price moves |
| Real estate | Pre-sales, collections, launches, inventory, approvals, execution, project debt, cash conversion | Project NAV/DCF; accounting revenue can lag sales and cash |
| REIT / InvIT | Occupancy/utilization, distribution coverage, leverage, tenant/counterparty concentration, lease/concession duration | NAV, sustainable distributions and rate sensitivity; do not equate all distributions with recurring earnings |
| Telecom | Subscribers, ARPU, churn, traffic, spectrum obligations, capex, competition | Lease/spectrum-consistent EV/EBITDA and cash flows; distinguish EBITDA from distributable cash |

For every sector report: map value chain and substitutes; explain market size
method and uncertainty; assess regulation, competition, entry barriers, demand,
supply/capacity and cycle; compare relevant peers and valuation history; identify
leading indicators, catalysts, beneficiaries and vulnerable businesses. Separate
sector attractiveness from attractiveness at prevailing stock valuations.

Company ranking should not be a universal P/E league table. Use common reporting
periods, business mix, sustainable returns, financial risk, and growth durability.
Explain exclusions and unavailable metrics. Comparisons across different
consolidation or lease bases must be reconciled or labeled non-comparable.
