# Indian IPO due diligence

Use the latest applicable RHP/final prospectus and amendments; DRHP-only research
is preliminary. Track the superseded version, effective date, issuer identity,
mainboard/SME platform, issue timetable and changes. Verify current issue-specific
category, lot, application, settlement and trading details against authoritative
documents. SEBI filing is not investment approval.

## Business and forensic work

Read business, industry, risk factors, restated financials and notes, management,
capital structure, litigation, related parties, objects of issue, basis for offer
price, and selling shareholders. Record page references. Investigate customer and
supplier concentration, recent margin/growth spikes, negative operating cash flow,
working-capital build, dependence on promoter entities, audit changes, contingent
liabilities, and pre-IPO transactions. Test how much fresh capital genuinely
reaches the company versus selling shareholders. OFS proceeds do not fund growth.

Analyze at least all available restated annual periods and interim comparables.
Keep restated versus historical reported accounts explicit. Do not mechanically
annualize a seasonal interim period. Track the issuer's own KPIs and any unusual
definitions, changing segments, pro forma adjustments, or short operating history.

## Capitalization and valuation

Distinguish face value from issue price. Reconcile pre-issue diluted shares plus
newly issued shares (including relevant conversion/ESOP dilution) to post-issue
diluted shares. OFS transfers existing shares and adds no shares. Explain the
denominator used for EPS, including pro forma versus weighted-average EPS.

At each price-band endpoint, compute fresh shares if the fresh amount is fixed,
post-issue market capitalization, net cash/debt after expenses and specified
deployment, P/E only for positive relevant earnings, and sector-appropriate
multiples. Cash used to repay debt must not be counted twice. Relate valuation to
comparable listed businesses and conservative scenarios, not GMP.

## Subscription and issue mechanics

Timestamp QIB, NII and retail categories as applicable, reserved portions, anchors,
and final versus provisional updates. Identify the denominator and treatment of
anchors and market-maker reservations before aggregating exchange figures.
Never double-count combined NSE/BSE bids or infer allotment probability as simply
1/subscription. Use official basis-of-allotment documents when published.
Inspect changes, extensions, withdrawals, selling shareholder incentives,
lock-ins, and upcoming supply. Verify the actual calendar and platform rules.

## GMP is mandatory by default

Every report includes a GMP table even if no usable quote exists. Attempt current
attributable publisher quotes without waiting for the user to request GMP.
Two sources are preferred where available; disclose if they appear syndicated or
only one can be accessed. Do not suppress available single-source evidence, but
label its limitations. Preserve negative premiums, explicitly quoted zero,
date-only times, unavailable values, and conflicts. No fabricated trend history.

Columns: publisher/direct link, quoted premium ₹, applicable offer price/band,
GMP %, observation time and precision, retrieval time, freshness, recent history,
and caveats. Formula: premium / explicitly selected price × 100. If final price
is unknown, label an upper-band illustration; do not call it the final price.
Issue-price-plus-GMP is an illustrative implied price, not an expected listing
price. Quotes are unofficial; they do not establish trade volume or true demand.

Once listed, show GMP as historical/not applicable to current trading and use
actual listing performance. An unavailable quote is null, never zero. Stale or
undated quotes cannot support a current listing-sentiment verdict. Do not blend
GMP into a fundamental score.

## Separate conclusions

Give a long-term ownership verdict and timeframe with valuation, thesis,
downside and review triggers. Give a separate listing-speculation assessment
with subscription quality, GMP reliability, market conditions and liquidity;
use “insufficient evidence” when needed. Discuss SME liquidity, concentration,
market-making arrangements and restrictions specifically. After listing, entry
zones use actual price history; do not invent pre-listing technical support.
